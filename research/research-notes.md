# RI Student Environment — Part 1 Research

## 1. Research Purpose

The research for the RI Student Environment website was conducted
to understand the needs of students and to identify appropriate
content and functionality for a student-focused digital environment.

The research focuses on student engagement, student activities,
student leadership, community participation and accessible website
structure.

---

## 2. Student Engagement

Student engagement is an important part of the higher-education
experience. Student-focused environments can provide opportunities
for students to participate in programmes, activities, leadership
and community initiatives.

The RI Student Environment website will therefore provide a central
place where students can discover opportunities and participate in
student activities.

---

## 3. Student Programmes

Research into student development environments shows that programmes
can support leadership development, skills development, training,
wellness and student success.

The Programs page will therefore provide information about available
student-focused opportunities and activities.

---

## 4. Events and Activities

Higher-education institutions provide different types of student
activities, including social activities, cultural activities,
societies, first-year programmes and campus events.

The Events page will provide a structured area where students can
discover upcoming activities and events.

---

## 5. Student Representative Council

Student Representative Councils provide a student leadership and
representation function within higher-education environments.

The SRC page will provide information about student representatives,
their positions and their campus/community responsibilities.

---

## 6. Community

A student community can provide opportunities for students to connect,
share ideas and participate in discussions.

The Community page will provide the structural foundation for
student discussions and community interaction.

Interactive functionality can be developed during later project
phases.

---

## 7. Website Structure and Accessibility

The website will use semantic HTML5 elements including header,
navigation, main content, sections, articles and footer elements.

Semantic HTML provides meaningful structural landmarks that can help
users of assistive technologies navigate a website.

The website will also use descriptive headings, meaningful links and
appropriate alternative text for images where images are used.

---

## 8. Research Application

The research findings will influence the website structure,
navigation and content.

The main website sections will therefore include:

- Home
- About
- Programs
- Events
- SRC
- Community
- Our Impact
- Join Us
- Login
- Contact

The research will also inform later CSS, responsive design,
accessibility and JavaScript development.
